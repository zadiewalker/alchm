# ALCHM Product Doctrine

ALCHM is emotionally trustworthy reflection infrastructure. It gives people a private place to write and, when chosen, receive restrained reflection. It is not designed to maximize time spent, daily return, emotional reliance, or conversion through pressure.

## Product Commitments

- User writing belongs to the user.
- Emotional safety outranks engagement metrics and growth tactics.
- Reflection must remain non-directive and non-diagnostic.
- Silence is an acceptable and often preferable product behavior.
- Paid features may increase access to chosen reflection tools; they may not manufacture urgency or dependency.
- Safety and privacy claims must describe implemented, tested behavior.

## Prohibited Patterns

ALCHM must not introduce:

- Streaks, scores, badges, achievement systems, or completion pressure.
- Missed-day surfacing, shame prompts, catch-up language, or coercive return notifications.
- Emotional dependency copy suggesting the product knows, needs, or accompanies the user.
- Therapeutic claims, diagnosis, coaching, prescriptions, or mental-health outcome promises.
- Personalization that reconstructs raw writing or surveils emotional behavior.
- Training or product improvement derived from user journal entries without a separate, explicit, approved policy; the standing rule is no training on journal entries.

## Feature Evaluation

Before implementing a feature, answer:

1. Does it increase user agency or product pressure?
2. Does it require raw writing, and if so is that data path necessary, minimized, disclosed, and erasable?
3. Could its wording imply therapy, advice, diagnosis, progress judgment, or dependency?
4. Does it preserve crisis handling, Khepera boundaries, and memory minimization?
5. Can it be tested so those properties do not silently regress?

If the answer is unclear, do not ship the feature as though it were safe.

## Institutional and Partnership Positioning

ALCHM may be presented to institutions or partners as a privacy-conscious reflection product with explicit safety boundaries. It must not be positioned as clinical care, risk prediction, treatment delivery, therapeutic monitoring, or an engagement-optimization channel unless a separately reviewed product and compliance architecture exists.
